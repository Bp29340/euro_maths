Turning a gardening problem into a quadratic function, then reading the answer off the parabola.

“How large a garden can you enclose with only 36 m of fencing?”

36 m of fencing x = width L = length
02The problem

The problem

The wall replaces one of the four sides, so only three sides have to be fenced.

Fencing available
36 m
shared between three sides
The constraint
2x + L = 36
03Modelling

From geometry to algebra

The area is written with one single variable, the width x.

1
2x + L = 36
the fencing
2
L = 36 − 2x
length in terms of x
3
A(x) = x × L
area of a rectangle
4
A(x) = x(36 − 2x)
substitute
Expanding gives a quadratic function
A(x) = −2x2 + 36x
04A first try

A first try: a width of 4 m

Before looking for the best garden, let us test one width and see what the function gives.

1
A(4) = −2 × 4² + 36 × 4
substitute x = 4
2
A(4) = −32 + 144
3
A(4) = 112
area for a 4m width, m²
Area for a 4 m width
112 m²

A garden of 4 m × 28 m uses all 36 m of fencing.

112 m² is already large — but nothing says it is the best. Changing the width changes the area, so the question becomes: which width wins?

05Solving

Which widths give an area of 130 m²?

This time the area is known and the width is not, so we solve a quadratic equation.

1
−2x² + 36x = 130
2
x2 − 18x + 65 = 0
divide by −2
3
Δ = b² − 4ac = 324 − 260 = 64
4
x = 18 ± √64 2 = 18 ± 82
x1 = 5 | x2 = 13
5 × 26
narrow and long · 130 m²
13 × 10
wide and short · 130 m²

Two different gardens share the same area. They sit either side of the summit, and their midpoint is exactly 9 — a first clue about where the maximum hides.

06The function

The area function

Its graph is a parabola. Because a is negative, it opens downwards — so it has a highest point.

Area of the garden, in m²
A(x) = −2x2 + 36x
a coefficient of x2 −2
b coefficient of x 36
c constant term 0

Only widths between 0 and 18 m make sense: beyond that, there is no fencing left for the length.

07The vertex

Finding the vertex of the parabola

The vertex gives the width that makes the area as large as possible.

xv = −b2a
→
xv = −362 × (−2)
→
xv = −36−4 = 9
Best width
9 m

The same 9 that sat halfway between the two solutions of the previous step.

08The length

Back to the length

Putting x = 9 back into the constraint from step 1 gives the second dimension.

1
L = 36 − 2x
2
L = 36 − 2 × 9
Length
L = 18 m
3
A(9) = 9 × 18 = 162
the maximum area, in m²
10Result

The best possible garden

Maximum area
162 m²
x = 9 m L = 18 m

36 m of fencing → 162 m² of maximum area — a shape twice as long as it is wide.
